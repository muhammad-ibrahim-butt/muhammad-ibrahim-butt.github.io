My role & delivery

I built the C# WinForms workflow that imports QuickBooks transactions, enriches item data, applies configured state-specific tax rules, identifies incomplete records, and exports reports for accounting review.

The work covered the QuickBooks-to-MySQL data flow, item and state lookups, calculation fields, correction screens, Excel exports, and creation of tax invoices back in QuickBooks.

The problem

The accounting workflow combined invoice and bill data with product measurements and state-specific rules. Spreadsheet-based preparation required users to reconcile those inputs repeatedly. Missing unit measurements, pack quantities, or tax values could leave an apparently complete report with incomplete calculations.

Three engineering decisions

1. Keep transaction data and tax rules separate

The application imports transactions through the QuickBooks Desktop SDK/QBFC15 and stores structured data in MySQL. Item records provide measurements and quantities; a state-and-item lookup supplies the configured tax values. Users can maintain those inputs without changing application formulas for every reporting run.

The configuration supports the client's reporting workflow. Report correctness still depends on the maintained rules and source data being appropriate for the reporting period.

2. Make incomplete records visible before export

I added dirty-record detection and correction workflows for calculation-critical fields. Accounting users can filter by state, category, or item, inspect missing data, update the item master or tax values, and rerun the calculation.

This connects validation to a practical correction path. A missing measurement is something the user can find and resolve before producing an accounting report.

3. Reuse prepared data across reports

Bulk loading and cached transaction data support monthly processing. Grouped views provide totals by company, invoice, customer, or vendor. Excel exports include TT101-style reporting and customer-level summaries, using the same enriched transaction data.

The application can also create calculated tax invoices in QuickBooks and retain their references to avoid duplicate processing.

Reporting workflow

  1. Select the company, reporting dates, and state.
  2. Import invoices or bills from QuickBooks.
  3. Match line items to product attributes and configured state tax values.
  4. Review flagged records and correct missing inputs.
  5. Recalculate totals and export the selected report for review.

Outcome

The delivered application turns recurring spreadsheet preparation into a repeatable desktop process. It centralizes the client's configured tax values and gives accounting users a visible route from imported records to corrected data and exported reports.

The broader finance-automation workflow now processes 10,000+ invoices per month. Monthly state-specific sales-tax preparation previously involved a team of 4–5 people working for about a week; the automated calculation now takes 5 minutes or less of processing time.

Implementation
