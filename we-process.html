The problem

A legal service instruction passes through several people: the client submits documents, an administrator assigns the work, and a process server records attempts and evidence in the field. The platform needed to connect these steps with document generation and billing while handling incomplete intake data and external-service failures.

Three engineering decisions

1. Model the service lifecycle explicitly

I organized the backend around service instructions, assignments, attempts, evidence, and status changes. Clients, process servers, and administrators use separate authentication guards and role-specific routes. Request validation, policies, services, and resources keep access rules and business transitions separate from response formatting.

This matters when an instruction is reassigned, put on hold, completed, or archived: the backend must coordinate the instruction, its field records, and the actions available to each role.

2. Gate AI extraction on required fields

Signed Salesforce webhooks create instructions and attach documents. When required details are missing, background jobs download and convert the documents, extract text with Azure Document Intelligence, and request structured fields from Azure OpenAI.

IntakeValidate the webhook, attach documents, and identify missing fields.
ExtractionConvert files, extract text, and store structured fields with extraction status and confidence.
Release checkMove complete instructions to NEW; keep incomplete instructions ON_HOLD and notify the relevant users.

Partial document failures are logged so usable results from other documents can still be processed. A scheduled check identifies stuck extraction work. The release check keeps incomplete intake visible before field work begins.

3. Tie billing and documents to operational events

Statement of Service PDFs are generated from recorded attempts, outcomes, and signatures. Invoice lines account for the service type, attempts, mileage, waiting time, document count, legal-aid rules, VAT, and discounts. Invoice-number creation uses database locking and retry handling.

Stripe payment workflows, invoice reminders, document conversion, and notifications sit alongside queues and scheduled jobs. This lets slow or recurring work run outside the immediate request-response flow.

Validation & production support

The project includes feature and unit tests around authentication, profiles, invoices, chat, and service controllers. Operational tools cover logging, failed extraction, storage cleanup, location-record retention, invoice exports, and data backfills. These support the platform after deployment as its workflows and records evolve.

Outcome

Client intake, administrator control, field evidence, generated documents, and billing run through one connected backend. Salesforce intake reduces repeated entry; extraction status exposes incomplete documents; and role-specific APIs give each portal the operations it needs.
